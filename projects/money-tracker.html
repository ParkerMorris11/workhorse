Money Tracker

A personal iPhone spending tracker. It connects bank accounts through Plaid, keeps transactions synced on the server, and turns them into dashboards, budgets, and a heatmap of daily spending.

TypePersonal iOS app
StackSwiftUI, Supabase (Postgres, Edge Functions), Plaid
RoleDesigned and built
01 / The design decision

The app never talks to Plaid.

Every Plaid call needs a secret, and anything shipped inside an iPhone app can be pulled out of it. So the app holds only its Supabase login. Server functions hold the Plaid secret and each bank's access token, which is stored encrypted with AES-GCM.

03 / How it works

Synced, categorized, and locked down.

  1. 01

    Three ways to stay synced

    Plaid's webhook triggers a sync when new transactions are ready. Pull to refresh asks the server to sync first. A scheduled job on my Mac syncs every bank with a job secret, not a login.

  2. 02

    Security rules, tested

    Postgres row-level security keeps each user to their own rows. The app can change only user_category on a transaction. pgTAP tests cover the rules and the views.

  3. 03

    Dashboard-ready views

    The app reads views, not raw tables: spending_transactions, daily_spending, monthly_category_spending, and budget_progress.

  4. 04

    Local AI categories

    Merchant rules run first. A model running in Ollama on my Mac (gemma4:e4b) suggests categories for the rest. Nothing is sent to a cloud AI.

  5. 05

    Ask, on the phone

    On iOS 26, Ask is a chat with Apple's on-device model. Its tools read my own spending data from Supabase, and the chat stays on the phone.

  6. 06

    Locked on launch

    Sign in with Apple or email through Supabase Auth, with the session in the Keychain. The app locks with Face ID on launch, with a passcode fallback.
